The Private Ending

You can guess an ending. Can someone else’s private ending change your answer?

By Dean Ammons1 minute to readNovember 13, 2026
Read the exampleRelated research

The thought experiment explained

Imagine that you have read every chapter of Harry Potter except the ending. You can predict a plausible finale. Getting it right would not, by itself, show that you saw the missing chapter.

Now imagine that another reader privately supplies that ending to the same information system. You ask the same question as before. Does the private information change your answers or help you identify the actual ending?

Paper II turns the analogy into a controlled construction: keep a synthetic public story fixed and independently assign one of M equally likely private endings. Under the stated isolation conditions, the guessing baseline is 1/M. A systematic gain would show source dependence in that experiment; further investigation would still have to establish the route.

Editorial summary of the manuscript’s Harry Potter analogy and controlled synthetic-ending construction; not a verbatim story. Paper II, p. 3 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24202383

What changes when the public story stays the same but the private ending changes?

The comparison varies a private source value while holding the recipient’s authorized information fixed. A source-dependent answer can carry information without repeating the private material.

How the paper develops the question

An explanatory summary of the thought experiment in Paper II.

This is a mathematical experimental construction. The public novel is an analogy, not a valid tracing test because prior exposure and predictable plot structure can explain a correct answer. The paper reports no measured cross-user leakage in a deployed service.
