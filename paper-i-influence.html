Paper I · Influence

Ammons LLM Preamble

A probability framework for behavioral influence through domain content

Dean AmmonsNovember 12, 2026Research preprint
Read the full paperPublication & DOI
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24177478
OverviewMain argumentsEvidence & limitsSources & citation

In plain language

Can a source change an answer without being quoted?

A model can use an idea without repeating its wording. The first paper asks how to measure that influence: compare what the model does with a specified work against what it does under a matched control, then evaluate the meaning of its responses. The aim is a testable measure of behavioral change, with explicit limits on what that change can tell us about a source.

Measure changes in meaning

The paper groups responses by their operational meaning: which inputs they use, what result they produce, and which domain and conditions apply. A renamed variable or a paraphrase can count as the same outcome; an invalid answer remains an outcome that must be counted.

Influence is the change between the exposed and control distributions over those outcomes. The Ammons Influence Probability asks how often a query drawn from a declared population has a change larger than a chosen threshold. It does not assign a probability that one observed answer came from a particular hidden historical source.

Source: manuscript, p. 3 · Sections 3.1, 3.2

Keep uptake, background support and discrepancy separate

The proposed pooling model combines the control response with a reference distribution representing the work's implications. Its predicted effect is incorporated evidence divided by total effective information, multiplied by the work's semantic difference from the control.

Within that model, weaker relevant background support increases the effect when uptake and discrepancy are held fixed. The conditions matter: a system can ignore a work, fail to interpret it, or already produce the same answers. Unfamiliar notation, rarity or a large document count does not determine any of those quantities by itself.

Source: manuscript, p. 5 · Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4

Distinguish a changed answer from useful learning

A response distribution can change because the model adopts the work, rejects it, becomes distracted or makes new errors. The paper therefore separates overall influence from improvement on consequences that the work actually supports.

It also separates two exposure routes. An encounter experiment supplies the work in the current context while keeping model parameters fixed. A training experiment changes the model through a specified learning process and evaluates it later without the work in context. Evidence for the first route does not establish the second.

Source: manuscript, p. 2 · Sections 1, 3.2, 5, 9

Give the model a clear way to fail

The scalar pooling law restricts the exposed response distribution to the line segment between the control and the work's reference distribution. An observed distribution outside that segment, beyond measurement error, rejects that approximation even if task accuracy improves.

The proposed calibration study varies support, content and dose separately, uses matched irrelevant and changed-rule controls, and evaluates held-out task families. Repeated contexts are needed to estimate response distributions. Effective background support and evidence uptake need their own calibration; fitting their ratio does not reveal a hidden training-document count.

Source: manuscript, p. 7 · Sections 4.4, 7.4, 9, 10

What the paper reports

Mathematical framework and an encounter-time pilot

The paper combines proofs and exact rational checks with a separate pilot on newly specified arithmetic microdomains. The mathematical checks verify the stated equations; the pilot tests whether the evaluated interface uses the supplied rules.

  • Twelve paired microdomains were tested in 24 fresh contexts. Each context answered one direct and one nested-composition query. Target-work exposure produced 24/24 correct answers; the matched irrelevant-rule condition produced 0/24. The analysis unit was the 12 domain pairs, rather than 48 independent answers.
  • The paper reports exact rational checks of the pooling factorization, threshold conditions, conditional scarcity comparison and approximation-error bounds. These are checks of stipulated mathematics, separate from model observations.

Scope and limits

  • The control note did not contain the queried rule. The result demonstrates encounter-time use of supplied information across instances of one function class; it does not establish persistent learning, a training update or historical source attribution.
  • One response per condition at each query does not estimate the full response distribution, identify background information mass and uptake separately, or fit the scarcity curve.
  • The interface did not expose an exact model release, decoding temperature, seed or backend checkpoint. The manuscript retains the issued prompts and responses, but a repeat with a versioned model and controlled decoding is needed for broader reproducibility.
  • The anonymous mathematical comparison corpus supports measurement design and dependence-aware balancing. Its deterministic comparisons are not additional LLM trials.

Inspect the original methods, results, and stated assumptions in the PDF.

Questions that would move the research forward

  1. Does the exposed response distribution stay on the proposed control-to-reference line segment across held-out task families?

    This directly tests the pooling law instead of treating any accuracy gain as confirmation.

  2. When relevant background support changes, can uptake, task difficulty and the work's discrepancy from the control be measured separately?

    The scarcity result holds those other factors fixed; an uncontrolled rarity correlation answers a different question.

  3. What remains after the work is removed from context, under a versioned model and a declared memory or training condition?

    This distinguishes immediate use from any claim of durable learning.

Bring an objection or proposed test to the public discussion.
