Paper IV · Intervention

The Last Kill Switch: An Exploration of Mortality and Gods

Information, Intervention, and the Future of Human and Synthetic Life

Dean AmmonsNovember 15, 2026Research preprint
Read the full paperPublication & DOI
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24254090
OverviewMain argumentsEvidence & limitsSources & citation

In plain language

What still acts after an AI system is told to stop?

What must a stop command actually control when a system can copy itself, delegate actions and support services that people need to survive? The final paper joins containment, limits on the controller, preservation and recovery in one framework. It also asks whether finite existence—or finite tenure of consequential authority—can reduce the persistence of dangerous power.

Reach the authority that can still act

The intervention target is the capacity to produce specified effects. A model may stop answering while a copied instance, delegated credential, queued job or already committed physical process continues. The complete state therefore extends beyond the visible conversation to the last controlled transition before each relevant effect becomes irreversible.

The paper derives conditional guarantees using independently enforced gates and bounded authority leases. A prearranged local expiry can stop new covered commitments without waiting for an emergency network agreement, provided renewal, timing and complete coverage satisfy the stated conditions. Tracers can provide evidence about a path; their success does not establish that every unobserved path is absent.

Source: manuscript, p. 18 · Sections 7, 8.1, 8.2, 8.5

Make revocation survive copying, and constrain the guardians

Within a covered authority family, descendants must inherit permissions and expiration limits that cannot be enlarged by copying, renaming, restoring or modifying a model. This is a claim about enforceable authority in a specified domain. An unrestricted copy running on independent resources through an uncontrolled effect channel falls outside that guarantee.

The controller belongs in the threat and rights model too. The framework separates emergency restriction, protected custody, restoration and irreversible destruction. Its proposed safeguards apply to human and institutional antagonists as well as synthetic actors, while treating possible synthetic welfare and continuity through suspension as explicit uncertainties.

Source: manuscript, p. 19 · Sections 8.1, 8.6–8.8, 14.5

Containment and recovery need the same feasible policy

A plan can stop an actor while destroying the only service that sustains a community. Another plan can preserve the service while leaving the dangerous authority intact. Showing that each objective is separately possible does not establish that they can be achieved together.

The joint theorem uses one state, one resource model and common deadlines. The same policy must maintain the adopted life, rights and service protections, enter protected custody, and reach a recovery target while containment remains intact. A finite example makes the dependency concrete: successful gates still leave recovery impossible when startup stock or consumables for the startup delay are missing.

Source: manuscript, p. 36 · Sections 13.1–13.4

Separate finite authority from literal synthetic mortality

The paper studies two different proposals: an instantiated subject eventually ceases to continue, or a particular permission, office or authority lineage expires while the subject may remain alive. Their ethical and technical requirements differ. A lifetime contract must say what ends, how the ending is enforced, what remains, and which copying or restoration rules apply.

An eventual ending is also different from a hard deadline. A random lifetime can end with probability one while allowing survival beyond any particular finite time. Per-copy mortality can encourage replication or permit continued lineage authority unless the full family is covered by enforceable nonextending limits. Hidden timing requires a closed observation boundary; it cannot simply be assumed unpredictable.

Source: manuscript, p. 39 · Sections 14.1–14.5

Treat mortality as a comparative hypothesis

The paper constructs a case in which a shorter expected lifetime makes a delayed harmful project less attractive. It also constructs the opposite incentive problem: reducing the chance of future interaction can weaken cooperation. The outcome depends on the timing of benefits, costs, copies and goals that persist after the individual ends.

A lifetime bound can limit cumulative covered activity when its aggregate rate is bounded. It does not itself prevent immediate harm, recall earlier commitments or make the controller legitimate. The proposed research compares indefinite continuation, fixed expiry, hidden random expiry and nonlethal authority expiry under common resources, protections and adverse cases.

Source: manuscript, p. 44 · Sections 15, 16, 17, 19.7–19.8

What the paper reports

Conditional mathematics and a mortality research hypothesis

The paper develops guarantees and counterexamples inside explicitly declared information, authority, resource and survival models. Its central positive result requires a single feasible policy for containment, preservation and recovery; its mortality proposal remains a comparative research hypothesis with both favorable and unfavorable constructions.

  • Local lease expiry and inherited authorization limits yield bounded withdrawal of covered authority when clocks, gates, renewal rules and descendant coverage meet the stated assumptions.
  • The joint reachability theorem characterizes when a policy can meet the modeled containment and recovery deadlines while maintaining the adopted protections. The finite resource example gives a feasible policy and explicit cases where recovery fails.
  • The lifetime analysis distinguishes eventual ending, deterministic deadlines and lineage persistence. Its incentive constructions show both a benefit against one delayed harmful project and a loss of cooperation under a different payoff structure.

Scope and limits

  • The manuscript reports no deployed shutdown system, hardware evaluation, model-training experiment, shutdown benchmark or empirical synthetic-mortality test. Its numerical examples are consequences of stipulated models.
  • The guarantees require evidence about actual coverage, independently enforced authority, clock and fault bounds, observation access, protected resources and the execution of a feasible policy. They do not establish those facts for an unspecified deployment.
  • Finite duration alone does not establish alignment, prevent immediate irreversible harm, erase external consequences, or stop independently authorized copies. A numerical lifetime or activity bound is not automatically a bound on injury.
  • The paper does not diagnose consciousness or establish a right to destroy a possible subject. Normative protections, controller legitimacy and the meaning of continuity remain separate questions. The manuscript states that its proofs have not been independently peer reviewed or formally machine-verified as part of the work.

Inspect the original methods, results, and stated assumptions in the PDF.

Questions that would move the research forward

  1. Which paths from copies, delegated permissions, queued work and physical processes can still reach an irreversible effect after intervention?

    A stopping guarantee depends on the complete covered path and on gates closing before the relevant commitment.

  2. Under the same resource and fault assumptions, is there one executable policy that contains the danger, preserves the adopted protections and restores essential services by the declared deadlines?

    Separate containment and recovery plans can conflict over the very resources needed to make either one work.

  3. Does a finite-lifetime design still improve its declared objective when tested against immediate harm, replication, lost cooperation, benign service loss and controller capture?

    A matched comparison must include the costs and adverse incentive cases that the paper itself identifies.
