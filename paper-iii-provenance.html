The Little Girl Who Gave the World Fire

Information Influence and Provenance Framework

Dean AmmonsNovember 14, 2026Research preprint
Read the full paperPublication & DOI
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24223543
OverviewMain argumentsEvidence & limitsSources & citation

In plain language

Can knowledge survive while the record of its source disappears?

A girl teaches a machine to make fire. Generations later, machines retain the skill but no longer possess the evidence identifying her. The fictional story opens a precise research question: which parts of a contribution can survive, which can be recovered, and what records are needed to connect a useful capability to the person who supplied it?

A capability and its origin are different information targets

The framework separates seven questions: admission, causal influence, retained information, recoverable disclosure, historical origin, contribution to a chosen outcome, and legally supported rights. A system can satisfy one of these conditions while lacking the evidence required for another.

The story's teaching event is true within the fiction. A later model's account of that event is an observation to evaluate. Mathematical constructions show how a skill can remain available while its origin becomes indistinguishable from another history for a particular observer. Losing that evidence does not turn an inherited capability into proof of independent invention.

Source: manuscript, p. 5 · Sections 1.2–1.4, 9, 11

Feedback can carry information into later learning

Information can reach a training process through evaluations, preferences, selection decisions or teacher outputs even when the original document is absent. The lifecycle model follows those transformations, retained records and optimization steps as connected events.

The paper constructs an informative score channel and a complete admission bound. Closing the declared route blocks that route under its assumptions; omitted logs or selection effects remain separate paths to account for. In the model experiment, candidate-correctness scores deliberately encode the target bit, making the transmission mechanism explicit.

Source: manuscript, p. 17 · Sections 5.2, 7, 8, 18.2

Follow descendants when defining source removal

Deleting an original record after it has produced a summary, trained model or teacher payload can leave those descendants unchanged. A counterfactual in which the source was never admitted requires the chosen replacement rule to be propagated through the relevant descendants.

Independent rediscovery is different again. If another genuinely independent source can supply the same skill, removing the original may have little effect on the final capability even though the original teaching remains a historical event. The paper gives separate constructions for skill copying, origin-record erosion, persistent causation and independent substitution.

Source: manuscript, p. 18 · Sections 5.3, 10.2, 11, 18.6

Specify the contribution question before assigning a share

Sources can reinforce one another or provide redundant alternatives. The governing calculus compares complete source-admission interventions and includes their interactions. A Shapley allocation then assigns contributions to a particular declared utility under a particular set of coalition comparisons.

That allocation inherits the units and assumptions of its target. A contribution to benchmark performance is not automatically a monetary amount. Eligibility, rights ownership, the payment pool and the agreement governing payment are separate inputs; an influence score does not settle them.

Source: manuscript, p. 13 · Sections 4.3–4.5, 5.4, 12, 13

Preserve evidence suited to the claim

An origin record can preserve the connection between a contribution and its source even when the numerical model state cannot identify that origin. A useful record may bind acquisition, content, transformations and use to the actual computational artifact, while distinguishing recorded ancestry from estimated causal influence.

The guarantee depends on truthful acquisition, complete relevant coverage and intact bindings. A signature authenticates an attestation; it does not make the attestation true. An ingestion record does not prove necessity, and a missing record in an incomplete archive does not prove nonuse. Attribution also has its own permission boundary because revealing a source's identity can disclose confidential information.

Source: manuscript, p. 17 · Sections 5.1, 5.3, 10, 14

What the paper reports

Framework, exact constructions and restricted transformer adaptation

A controlled experiment used the pretrained Pythia-14m model with only 256 output-projection coordinates trainable. Eight independent source-assignment blocks compared source-derived feedback against matched independent replacement, followed by transfer through teacher scores to fresh students.

  • Mean unrestricted next-token accuracy on new wordings of the trained registry keys was 96.09% with both source blocks admitted and 57.81% with independent replacement. The control retained the same keys, templates and optimization budget; it was not an untrained model.
  • Fresh students trained from the source-adapted teachers' numeric output payloads reached the same reported mean accuracy, 96.09%, on the evaluated wordings. This measures transfer through a specified score channel.
  • Making external origin records available or withholding them while keeping numerical student inputs identical produced identical adapted rows. Replacing one source block and regenerating the teacher and student changed the descendants. These are distinct interventions.

Scope and limits

  • The evaluation uses new wordings of already trained keys. It does not test unseen keys, general problem-solving, full-model training or preserved language quality outside the registry task.
  • The candidate-correctness scores encode the answer bit completely. The experiment does not estimate how often arbitrary real-world feedback leaks private information.
  • The provenance comparison changes access to external records that are outside the numerical learner. It does not show the model learning and then forgetting a contributor's identity, reconstruct an unknown training history, or measure an origin-recovery decoder.
  • Removing source and provenance record views while retaining trained descendants is not secure storage erasure or model unlearning. The eight-block measurements are descriptive results for the declared task, rather than a population-wide leakage rate or a legal ownership finding.

Inspect the original methods, results, and stated assumptions in the PDF.

Questions that would move the research forward

  1. Which preserved observations distinguish the claimed historical origin from an independently acquired or observationally equivalent history?

    The answer identifies what an attribution system can actually establish for its observer.

  2. When a source is replaced, are its summaries, scores and learned descendants regenerated while genuinely independent substitutes remain governed by the declared policy?

    This makes the causal comparison match the claim about source removal.

  3. Do the measured transfer and attribution results persist with less informative feedback, unseen keys, broader adaptation and an explicit origin-recovery task?

    These tests extend the restricted experiment without treating its current result as a general benchmark.
