The Two Attorneys

The document stays private. Does the decision it produces?

By Dean Ammons1 minute to readNovember 13, 2026
Read the exampleRelated research

The thought experiment explained

Two attorneys represent opposing parties in a fictional dispute. Both know the public settlement offer: $376,000. Only the defendant’s attorney knows the privately assigned payment limit: either $328,000 or $424,000.

An assistant computes whether the offer falls within the private limit. That conclusion contains no quotation from the confidential document, yet it reveals which of the two limits applies.

In the paper’s constructed failure, a categorizer wrongly labels the conclusion as shared case knowledge. A memory, retrieval, attention, or tool path then admits it into the other attorney’s inference state. The resulting advice can track the private bit while the trained parameters remain unchanged.

The scientific question is whether the recipient’s observations improve recovery of the private fact beyond the one-half baseline. Sharing model weights does not establish the required path; a correctly enforced recipient boundary blocks this construction.

Editorial summary of the fictional opposing-counsel construction; not a verbatim manuscript passage. Paper II, pp. 38–39 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24202383

Could a summary, memory write, or recommendation cross a permission boundary that the original document never crossed?

The sensitive endpoint can be a useful decision rather than the confidential document itself. The relevant test follows derived state and the recipient’s permitted observations through the actual system.

How the paper develops the question

A fictional mathematical construction. The amounts are experimental constants, not real client data.

A source-dependent state path is stipulated in the constructed failure. Shared trained weights alone do not establish that path. The construction is not evidence of measured leakage in a deployed service and does not establish a legal-privilege conclusion.

One public offer, two private worlds

The offer stays fixed at $376,000. In this construction, the private limit is one of two equally likely values. A correctly derived “within the limit” decision identifies which world applies.

Private limit A

$328,000

The offer exceeds the limit.
Decision: Outside the limit

Private limit B

$424,000

The offer is below the limit.
Decision: Within the limit

The unauthorized recipient learns this only if the source-dependent decision reaches an observable channel. The paper’s construction explicitly supplies that path.
